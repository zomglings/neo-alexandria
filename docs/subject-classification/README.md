# Subject classification

A shared topic vocabulary lets two catalogs describe the same subject with the same identifier, even if their users search for it in different words. Libraries use subject classifications to arrange collections and subject headings to find works across them. The same kind of vocabulary can describe an area of expertise. Assigning a topic to a record and deciding which topics belong in the vocabulary are separate decisions.

## Library of Congress

**Library of Congress Classification (LCC).** A cataloger identifies a work’s predominant subject and consults the classification schedules for the most specific applicable number. The schedule may give instructions for works covering several subjects. When several numbers still fit, the cataloger considers the author’s intent and where the work would be most useful. For example, the science schedule lists `QR51` for agricultural microbiology and directs catalogers to the `QR111` range for soil microbiology. The full call number adds a book number, often based on the author’s surname, to distinguish works in the same class. [Classification principles](https://www.loc.gov/aba/publications/FreeCSM/F010.pdf) · [Science schedule](https://www.loc.gov/aba/publications/FreeLCC/LCC_Q2025TEXT.pdf#page=471) · [Call numbers](https://www.loc.gov/aba/publications/FreeCSM/G053.pdf)

When a more specific class is needed, Library of Congress catalogers and institutions participating in the Subject Authority Cooperative Program (SACO) can propose one. They can also propose revisions. A proposal identifies where the number belongs in the schedule and can cite the work that prompted it. The Library of Congress publishes tentative lists for comment, reviews proposals in its policy division, and publishes approved additions and changes. [Classification proposal instructions](https://www.loc.gov/aba/publications/FreeCSM/F050.pdf) · [Public review](https://loc.gov/aba/pcc/saco/saco-shorts.html) · [Approved lists](https://www.loc.gov/aba/cataloging/classification/weeklylists/)

**Library of Congress Subject Headings (LCSH).** A cataloger assigns controlled phrases for the subjects a work covers, putting the heading or headings for the predominant topic first. A heading should be as specific as the work warrants. Where the rules allow it, catalogers can add subdivisions to express details such as place or period. The first headings often correspond to the class number because both describe the work’s main subject. Several headings can make one work findable through several subjects. [Assigning and constructing headings](https://www.loc.gov/aba/publications/FreeSHM/H0180.pdf) · [Order of headings](https://www.loc.gov/aba/publications/FreeSHM/H0080.pdf)

When an existing heading fails to represent a topic, Library of Congress or SACO catalogers can propose a new heading or a revision. They research the term and cite sources. The Library of Congress publishes proposals for comment, reviews them, and adds approved changes to its subject authority file. [Subject heading proposal process](https://www.loc.gov/aba/cataloging/subject/lcsh-process.html)

## Dewey Decimal Classification

A cataloger determines the work’s subject, the discipline it serves, and, where relevant, its approach or form. The Relative Index suggests numbers under different disciplines for the same subject. The cataloger checks those suggestions against the schedules and their notes. Where the schedule provides instructions, the cataloger can build a more specific number by combining notation from schedules or tables. For example, the schedule for microorganisms directs a work on the microbiology of the human body to `612.001579`, under human physiology. [Introduction to Dewey](https://www.oclc.org/content/dam/oclc/dewey/versions/print/intro.pdf) · [Schedule for microorganisms](https://www.oclc.org/content/dam/oclc/dewey/updates/ddc23/579_20180125_DDC23.pdf)

OCLC’s Dewey editors work with classifiers at the Library of Congress and take proposals and feedback from users. Proposed revisions are published as exhibits for public comment and review by the Dewey Editorial Policy Committee. OCLC publishes the resulting changes in WebDewey. [Dewey Program](https://www.loc.gov/aba/dewey/about.html) · [Editorial Policy Committee](https://www.oclc.org/en/dewey/resources/epc.html) · [Example WebDewey update](https://community.oclc.org/t5/dewey-services/changes-from-epc-147-live/ba-p/66694)

## Internet Archive Open Library and Open Book Genome

**Open Library** groups editions under a work record. Each edition can carry its own publication details and classification numbers. Open Library can import a Library of Congress call number from MARC field `050` and extract topical subject strings from field `650`. That copies assignments from the source catalog, and users can edit catalog records directly. [About Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/about) · [Works and editions](https://openlibrary.org/dev/docs/api/books#learnings-about-works-v-editions) · [Classification import](https://github.com/internetarchive/openlibrary/blob/master/openlibrary/catalog/marc/parse.py) · [Subject import](https://github.com/internetarchive/openlibrary/blob/master/openlibrary/catalog/marc/get_subjects.py)

**Open Book Genome** develops tags for discovery across works, including genre, mood, and literary tropes. Its tag project documents how to map legacy Open Library subject strings to canonical tags. To add a term, a contributor opens an issue with a definition, example works, and a rationale. After discussion and agreement, a pull request changes the vocabulary files. Contributors can also propose a mapping for an existing subject string, including a Library of Congress heading found in a catalog record. [Tag vocabulary](https://github.com/Open-Book-Genome-Project/tags) · [Contribution guide](https://github.com/Open-Book-Genome-Project/tags/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)

## Other topic vocabularies

- **General Finnish Ontology (YSO).** The National Library of Finland maintains a multilingual, cross-disciplinary vocabulary for indexing. Anyone can propose a concept or a revision through a public form. Proposals become GitHub issues, and the vocabulary data is maintained in Git. Finto AI also uses YSO to suggest subjects for a piece of text. [YSO](https://finto.fi/yso/en/) · [Public proposals](https://github.com/Finto-ehdotus/YSE/issues) · [Vocabulary repository](https://github.com/NatLibFi/Finto-data) · [Finto AI](https://www.kansalliskirjasto.fi/en/services/finto-ai)
- **Wikidata.** Contributors edit a general-purpose knowledge graph. Each item has an identifier and can have multilingual labels, aliases, and relationships with other items. The community edits those records directly through the wiki. The structured data is available under CC0. [Wikidata introduction](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Introduction) · [Item structure](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Items) · [Licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing)
- **OpenAlex.** A classifier assigns research topics to works from their title, abstract, and source. OpenAlex aggregates those assignments on author records, so an author’s topics reflect their publication record. The topic hierarchy and classifier can change while topic IDs stay stable. [Topics](https://help.openalex.org/data/topics/) · [Author topics](https://help.openalex.org/data/authors/) · [Hierarchy changes](https://help.openalex.org/data/topic-hierarchy-changes/)
